## Source-grounded conclusions

**ClockBound here is an active system-clock disciplinarian, not a passive chrony reader.** Upstream `clock-bound/src/daemon/clock_state.rs:143` selects `SystemClockDiscipliner::with_kernel_clock_adjuster` without `test-side-by-side`; `system_clock_discipliner.rs:37–40` constructs `KAPIClockAdjuster`; `system_clock_discipliner/ntp_adjtime.rs:24–28` calls actual libc `ntp_adjtime`, with smoothing/step operations at118–145. The accepted compiler explicitly excludes `test-side-by-side` (`scripts/governance_linux_compile.py:110–124`). A no-op adjuster would change the tested model.

Therefore the actual joined evaluation requires exclusive clock-steering ownership on the **new disposable host**: stop the default `chronyd` and any other active ntpd/systemd-timesyncd before starting this daemon, verify they are inactive and cannot automatically compete during the bounded test, and record that change. This is consistent with the pinned upstream `clock-bound/assets/clockbound.service:3–4`, which declares `Conflicts=ntpd.service systemd-timesyncd.service chronyd.service`. It requires a future explicit host/service/clock-change approval; nothing is stopped now. An operational decision to trust ordinary Amazon NTP does not mean leaving two independently steering daemons running. Do not stop time services on the developer Mac, runner, production or an existing host.

The upstream service cannot be installed verbatim: it points at the stock binary and contains automatic restart (`clockbound.service:10–12`). The evaluation must invoke its fixed source-bound entrypoint once and keep auto-restart off. The privileged clock writer is separate from the nonprivileged raw reader; no clock-setting privilege reaches Node or the worker.
