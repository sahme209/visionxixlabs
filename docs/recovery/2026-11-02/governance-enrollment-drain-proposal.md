## Minimal no-schema design (proposed)

Reuse existing durable workflow/event tables with **new strict versioned kinds**, exact immutable tenant/epoch/operation bindings and CAS/history validation. This avoids a database migration, not new protocol semantics. Review transaction/query/index bounds and storage retention before implementation; stop with unavailable on malformed/overflow/unreadable history. Do not repurpose existing active baseline pointers or change the approved native lease record format.

| Record | Proposed immutable identity and states | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Tenant transition | `governance_transition_v1`, deterministic tenant ID; epoch UUID; approved candidate/parent digest; reviewed manifest/qualification digests | `open` → `draining` → `activated`, or explicitly `aborted` with history. Epoch/version advance by CAS; no automatic reopening after timeout. A draining barrier refuses new uncovered mutation/credential reservations. It is not an active baseline or grant. |
| Outbound slot | `governance_outbound_v1`; tenant/epoch/server nonce; exact operation/attempt/profile, target/connection/source and issuer artifact identity | `reserved` → `submission_intent` → provider-bound observation, then `quiescent` only on supported evidence; `unknown` remains blocking. Store safe identifiers/digests, never bearer/body/secret. A confirmed request receipt may still represent active work. |
| Runtime/legacy qualification | `governance_runtime_qualification_v1`; tenant/epoch; exact fleet/issuer/credential generations; proof/source digests; verifier/version and evidence expiry | Immutable evidence candidate with genuine independent review where required. Never a client checkbox, self-reported heartbeat or operator-written “safe” boolean. Missing applicable population yields unavailable, not an empty set. |

Use the same organization advisory key as existing stores: `governance_org_` plus canonical tenant hash. Transition, all participating new outbound/credential reservations and final activation acquire it **first**, before operation/approval/session/connection locks. Stable lock order and bounded scans are acceptance requirements. Callbacks executed inside the lock are DB-only; provider/control-plane evidence is collected outside then locked exact bindings/freshness are rechecked.

### Reservation and actual submission

1. A current authorized caller reserves an exact outbound slot under the org lock **before** any consequential network submission or reusable write credential issuance. Existing operation approval/claim semantics remain mandatory. A slot grants no execution permission. Read-only reconciliation remains available with current reader authority while draining.
2. After all ordinary preparation, the real fixed transport atomically records `submission_intent` under the org lock, validates the current epoch/barrier and exact operation, and retains private one-use current authority/freshness through commit-result delivery and the last synchronous pre-fetch boundary. No awaited authorization remains after that final private check. Recording intent before network is essential: a crash/delay in the commit→fetch interval is visible to drain.
3. Provider IO runs outside locks. The slot remains blocking while the call may be submitted, accepted, queued, running, retried internally or ambiguous. It is never declared quiescent merely because local HTTP ended, a worker lease expired, a credential expired or request cancellation fired. If a final local check refuses before submission, only a supported exact no-submission protocol may close the slot; otherwise retain unknown. Do not grant another process authority to replay the private intent.
4. Receipt/recovery updates append exact bound provider observations without resending. A lost reply/commit acknowledgment leaves the original slot unknown/blocking indefinitely. Concurrent reconciliation is CAS-bound; wrong tenant/target/run/attempt or incomplete provider history cannot clear it. Existing consumed attempt/approval cannot reset or adopt another operation.
5. A positive provider acknowledgment is not drain completion. A deployment/engine run may continue or spawn retries/tasks; quiescence needs profile-specific terminal/no-future-effect evidence, or an explicitly qualified stop/revocation protocol plus readback. Cancellation acknowledgment alone does not prove effects stopped. Unknown profiles remain unsupported for activation.

A blocker opened before the barrier can race physically after it; activation must wait for that durable blocker rather than assuming the barrier cancelled it. Requests starting after the committed barrier refuse before new slots/credential minting. This converts an untracked network gap into an explicit blocker; it does not claim distributed atomicity or exactly-once delivery.

### Transition and activation

1. Obtain a reviewed exact candidate/parent and manifest, but do not activate. Proposed **Begin drain** authority uses existing capabilities: a current accepted administrator with `policy:manage` requests an exact plan binding tenant, transition epoch, candidate/parent digest, manifest/qualification digests, designated requester/executor and expiry. A genuinely independent current `agent:approve` reviewer, distinct from that requester and executor, reviews that exact plan; no model recommendation, baseline approval or historical review substitutes. The original administrator/requester must still hold current `policy:manage` and paired authority when deliberately beginning drain. Under the org lock, exact approval/expiry, expected version/epoch and one-attempt CAS are checked, the decision is consumed only for its original begin-drain operation, and `draining` is committed atomically. No new role or permission is introduced. These are proposed semantics requiring approval, not implemented behavior.
2. Drain verifier reads exact slots, credential lease blockers and qualified fleet/legacy evidence. Existing native lease counts stay independently validated: pending unknown blocks indefinitely; issued lifetime uses actual provider expiry/database clock, never guessed grace. An expired bearer closes only its future credential-use exposure; it does not prove a request accepted before expiry has finished.
3. Under the org-first activation transaction, recheck exact barrier epoch/state, candidate/parent, current administrator/reviewer/source/evidence, complete registered manifest, zero unqualified/active/unknown outbound work, credential blockers and current runtime qualification. All writers use the same barrier protocol; otherwise refuse. No provider call or waiting loop occurs under the lock.
4. Only then atomically update active baseline pointers and transition state. Actual enrollment still needs separately authorized activation/native workflow and genuine acceptance. If any bound proof expires while waiting or after final DB await, refuse without manufacturing a fresh proof or deleting history.
5. Proposed **Abort drain** is a separate operational action because reopening writes changes admission: a current administrator with `policy:manage` requests a fresh exact abort plan binding tenant, draining epoch/version, pre-existing admission/active-policy digest and intended restoration. A genuinely independent current `agent:approve` reviewer, distinct from the abort requester/executor, must approve that exact unexpired plan. The original administrator/requester deliberately executes with current paired `policy:manage` authority, one-attempt claim and exact epoch/version CAS; no begin-drain, baseline or old approval is reused. Abort restores only the bound pre-existing admission state and preserves any active policy; it cannot erase slots, unconsume attempts, imply quiescence, restore revoked provider credentials or weaken policy. If the bound state changed, refuse and obtain fresh review. Crash/restart remains draining until this authorized decision; unsupported/missing verifiers remain blocking for enrollment, including after abort. These semantics require approval before implementation.

## Mixed/older issuer qualification: required external proof

New code cannot force an old process to honor a new DB record. Therefore `draining` alone is insufficient while any old issuer/worker/caller remains able to write. Qualify the **whole population** before activation, not only instances that voluntarily registered.

- A trusted deployment/control-plane inventory must identify every active runtime/issuer, worker/cron/job, allowed alternate writer and credential source for the tenant, with exact immutable artifact/protocol identity. A heartbeat or process self-declaration is not proof no older instance exists. Unknown/unreachable instances block.
- Independently establish retirement/fencing of old issuers (including their routes, schedules, queues and reusable credential paths) and absence of their in-flight effects. This may require separately authorized deployment/network/provider operations and evidence. Container replacement or a current load-balancer version alone does not prove old requests/jobs/credentials are gone.
- For pre-ledger bearers, require provider-backed exact expiration/revocation evidence or a separately validated provider credential/installation-generation transition whose semantics actually invalidate all relevant old credentials. Do not assume key rotation, app reinstallation or account/session logout revokes issued tokens. No guessed one-hour wait, grace period, “old enough” timestamp or empty ledger proof.
- If a provider cannot enumerate/prove legacy validity or invalidate it comprehensively, that tenant/profile remains unqualified indefinitely. Document a separate provider-specific migration procedure for review; do not invent a positive generic path. Provider acceptance before credential invalidation still needs effect/run drain evidence.
- Bind fleet, credential and provider-effect proofs to the exact transition epoch/manifest and original observations, including scope/completeness/freshness. Customer/administrator attestation can record context but cannot replace a missing authoritative verifier. Rollback to a pre-protocol runtime must be prevented or invalidate qualification and refuse admission.

Proposed local TDD first, simulated provider boundaries clearly labeled; no new live authority is implied:

2. Real-clock deadlines/session loss during every last DB/commit/caller await and immediately before fetch; zero POST/PUT after refusal. Simulated provider must verify no database locks held across IO.
3. Accepted-long-running job stays blocking; known terminal evidence can clear only exact supported profile; wrong/partial/purged readback and cancellation-ack-only remain unknown. Lost POST/commit reply, worker crash and repeated recovery never resubmit or reset an attempt.
4. Credential cases: pending mint unknown blocks indefinitely; real issued provider expiry can end bearer exposure but not existing accepted-run blockers; malformed/overflow/unavailable histories refuse. No guessed expiry fixtures.
5. Mixed-runtime negatives: fresh empty ledger, missing old runtime, stale manifest, unsigned/self-reported artifact, unknown token population, key rotation without revocation proof and rollback to old issuer all refuse. A positive needs a real verifier contract plus provider-specific evidence, not hand-built “qualified=true”.

A single unregistered mutator keeps coverage empty/activation refused. Current catalog remains a review queue, not a safe manifest.
7. Native begin-drain/status/recovery/abort/activation workflow, genuine independent decisions and separately authorized real provider/runtime transition evidence. Preserve supported reads/recovery while mutations refuse; explain the blocking operation and safe next action without revealing secrets.
