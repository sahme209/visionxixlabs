## Native ingress must remain separate from origin control

`native/origin_broker.c:195–225` accepts exactly `start`, `observe`, `begin`, `ack`, `live`, with fixed dictionary key counts, per-peer busy state, bounded jobs, absolute deadlines and epoch capture. It has no business request envelope or application authorization. `end_peer` and job timeout revoke the origin slot for its bound origin/startup peer. Therefore a business request disconnect/cancel cannot simply be implemented by cancelling the origin connection: that would revoke the entire owned epoch. Conversely a business cancellation cannot reset an origin deadline, epoch, consumed bootstrap or startup reservation.

The existing request-channel proposal already selects a separate business Mach service and six data operations. Its exact native-to-Node delivery and cancellation bookkeeping must be specified before implementation. Do not add arbitrary payloads/actor fields to the origin protocol or broaden the captured zero-argument native assertion. Native sender filtering proves only the selected process boundary; the private handler still authenticates the opaque paired-session credential and resolves current durable user/session/tenant/membership/permissions.

`native/origin_guard.c:45–76` permits one bounded synchronous native probe, rejects concurrent probing, checks actual process/source identity and a fresh session assertion on its private native queue, and permanently revokes on timeout/failure. It exports only frozen `assertCurrentOrigin` and one-use `consumeBootstrapResources`. The ingress must deliver work into the owned canonical JS main without requiring a JS callback to answer that native challenge. It must not reuse the native probe queue as an unbounded business queue. Existing synchronous probe time is charged to handler/transaction deadlines, and abort/expiry must be checked after it returns; a pending JS timer alone is not proof that a monotonic deadline was respected.

## Request identity, cancellation and lifetime

- Bind each outstanding business request to its admitted transport connection, fixed envelope identifiers and owned runtime epoch. A connection-local cancellation affects only its own outstanding work; an untrusted request ID cannot cancel another connection's work. The six-operation JSON schema stays unchanged: cancellation bookkeeping is transport state, not a caller-selected authority extension.
- Distinguish the `cancel_execution` business action (existing plan/version/client-request binding and durable coordinator semantics) from transport cancellation/timeout. A transport loss aborts current dispatch eligibility and propagates the original request signal; it cannot assert that a submitted provider effect stopped, reset a claim or authorize a replay.
- Preserve strict4096-byte input, nested1024/256-byte bodies,262144-byte output, execution25s/history10s deadlines and existing rate policies. Bound outstanding requests and queued responses at the native-to-JS seam too. Reject late completion without adopting or retrying it. Exact admission limits must be selected in the ingress specification rather than implied by the origin broker's unrelated job limit.
- `privateReleaseMergeRequestData.ts` already races a handler against its effective abort signal, cancels late returned response bodies, bounds stream reads and releases readers. This is caller completion handling, not proof that arbitrary underlying database work stopped. Actual coordinator/store/native checks must still prevent forbidden later transitions and preserve unknown results.
- Current bootstrap exposes only `close()` and drops its graph/disconnects once; current canonical main closes immediately. A future dispatch lifetime must atomically stop admitting work before dropping graph references/disconnecting, abort owned outstanding requests, and prevent saved handler/lifetime references from dispatching after close. Describe bounded completion versus unknown before resource disposal; do not simply add a handler export to canonical public exports.
- Origin failure closes ingress and the whole private lifetime permanently. Business request failure alone need not invent a new native epoch. Neither event can turn a consumed operation/credential/intent into a fresh attempt.

## Preserve the existing durable effect seams

`privateReleaseMergeComposition.ts` captures private database/session/vault/auth/transport; its execution handler forwards the resolved expected identity into the real coordinator. Keep that binding when adding ingress. The history core remains free of coordinator/credential/provider dependencies. Do not replace it with a convenient native executor or import the global HTTP wrappers.

`mergeOutboundDrainCore.ts` calls the actual issuer reader, captures the original plan/signal/issuer for credential reservation, revalidates durable bindings under original org-first locks, and returns a once-consumable freshness closure after committed submission intent. `releaseMergeCredentialExposure.ts` separately governs durable exposure. `releaseProviderMergeTransport.ts:54–55` invokes intent and final authority freshness immediately before its sole PUT. Preserve both checks and the original claim/lease/intent ordering; no native IPC callback may directly submit a provider write. A fresh identity is not an actor, independent review, source/policy or enrollment grant.

The joined acceptance must distinguish loss before claim, after claim/before exposure, after intent/before PUT and after PUT/before confirmation. Measure each durable boundary; only the first necessarily has zero claim. A late native response or transport acknowledgement does not erase persisted uncertainty or remint credentials. Provider/credential responses may be simulated, while these actual compiled checks and database transactions remain real in the positive vertical.

## Private fixture and maintenance contracts

`origin_private_resource_supplier.m:107–122` checks current owner/installation/item policy and cancellation/deadline around each exact resource read, burns failed startup state and wipes partial material. Broker delivery binds the original epoch/child/nonces/deadline and leaves late blocking supplier storage alive until its worker returns. Preserve those properties; do not carry request data into resource selection or reconnect the supplier for each action.

The new administrative fixture helper is explicitly a proposed trusted material producer. Its private frame must bind the fixed fresh installation generation/target/helper and exact role order, bounded lengths and EOF; it must not become a generic datasource/SQL/command/credential API. Actual database owner/admin identity, fixed schema/roles/socket, password-auth runtime policy and logging controls remain needed definitions. A fixed source string or successful ordinary initialization cannot qualify them. The helper receives synthetic key material only within that new reviewed administrative boundary; it must never receive authority to operate production resources through inherited environment/global clients.

Distinguish fixture SQL transaction state from the existing three-Add/publish journal. SQL role/database creation may commit before any item exists, and item creation may succeed before a reply or locator publication. Define terminal failed/unknown phase records and new reviewed recovery; do not infer absence or repeat creation/Add from a missing success acknowledgement. A successful maintenance receipt neither changes runtime readiness nor arms an owner epoch.

## Review disposition

The adjacent identity/material designs preserve the original selected boundaries; no contradiction requiring an alternate mechanism was found. Two important refinements were sent to their owners during review:

1. **Resolved by source-only contract refinement:** the first exact fixture HBA allowed administrative peer access only to the not-yet-created qualification database, but its creation phase needs a connection to `postgres`. The exact fixture contract now defines the initial administrator via fixed `initdb --username`, root-only administrative `postgres` bootstrap peer access, and a separately recorded final-HBA publish/reload/new-connection refusal before item Adds. Runtime SCRAM access stays confined to the qualification database; reload uncertainty leaves an incomplete generation, no retry. No actual HBA/role operation was performed.
2. **Resolved by source-only contract refinement:** the refined ingress now requires fresh private-nonce request-scoped arbitration on the authenticated native lane, exact admitted id/consumed child/current connection/epoch, broker-side monotonic deadline and serialization against cancellation. A broker-terminal cancellation refuses even if child/JS delivery is delayed; ordinary origin-live alone is explicitly insufficient. External cancellation is bound to its original authenticated desktop connection; durable `cancel_execution` is separate. This does not remove the unavoidable race after the final successful check and remote submission. Actual lane/N-API/authentication behavior remains later qualification, not measured by the paragraph.

Both findings are resolved at design level. The joined `IMPLEMENTATION-REVIEW.md` is ready for a bounded local source/compilation/pure-mock decision; it explicitly excludes actual PostgreSQL/SQL, privileged helper/root/account/service, Security/Keychain, signing/mounting or custodian/provider execution. Unknown startup/enforcement/principal/policy facts still refuse, and source/mock positives do not qualify those facts. Before any actual operational bundle, bind one emitted consumer and all installed/provisioned facts and commands separately. The acceptance matrix retains native/current-main/cache/source and same-consumer adversarial checks. Fleet/legacy drain, production writer withdrawal, other mutation families and A9 disconnected deployment remain separate obligations, never delegated to a successful identity or one synthetic merge.
