# Original workflow diagram: brief and concepts

November5,2026 · design proposal only · no concept selected or prototype authorized by this document.

## Purpose and placement

Answer one evaluator question: **“What separates asking for a change from an action, and what can I inspect if the outcome is uncertain?”** The intended future placement is the Product page’s existing conceptual-workflow section, not a new hero animation or an additional competing panel. First evaluation would be an isolated local preview beside the existing composition; replacement or publication is a separate decision.

Grounding: [website architecture](axiom-website-information-architecture.md), [interface criteria](interface-clarity.md), [current illustration guide](knowledge/website-workflow-visual.md), and actual `components/marketing/WorkflowEvidenceMap.tsx` / `app/product/page.tsx`. Their current five labels are Repository context, Exact proposal, Independent review, Separate requester execution, Recorded outcome and recovery. Preserve their distinction between review and requester intent. The proposed “policy” explanation is **current prerequisites and operation-specific support**, not an invented automatic policy verdict or a sixth implemented control. No backend schema, permission or existing help-ID contract changes follow.

Use the brief’s progression as a teaching outline: request/context → exact proposal and current prerequisites → independent review where supported → separate deliberate requester execution where supported → recorded outcome and recovery. Show that any unmet prerequisite can stop progression; an unknown outcome does not advance to a success state or loop back into execution.

## Shared content and truth boundary

Persistent caption: “Conceptual illustration · local explanation only.” Keep current source qualifications visible: “Check your installed version and enabled workflow for available controls” and “Governance Intelligence remains incomplete; this illustration does not establish activation or action coverage.” These are explanations, not account-derived statuses.

The final explanation presents three **possible outcome categories**, not sample operation results: confirmed evidence, refused, and unresolved unknown. “Verified outcome” may describe evidence to inspect, but must never be the automatic terminal label. A recovery plan is not proof of recovery. No default green checkmark, fabricated policy pass, reviewer identity, consent checkbox, connected integration or successful deployment. “Implemented,” “Local preview” and “Planned” retain the architecture’s scoped meanings; the future pilot remains a local preview and cannot promote any operation to implemented.

## Three alternatives for later explicit selection

1. **Evidence rail.** Five quiet, flat record cards run left to right and stack vertically on phones. A small prerequisite annotation belongs to the proposal; distinct Review and Requester intent cards prevent conflation. Selecting a record reveals its explanation, with an always-visible final fork for confirmed/refused/unknown and a read-only recovery note. Strongest continuity with the existing Product surface and clearest reading order; least novel. Its risk is visual density, so keep each card to one label and one short sentence. No animated travelling token that implies an operation occurred.

2. **Separated handoffs.** A single exact-proposal sheet sits between three text-labelled responsibilities: requester, independent reviewer, and supported executor/workflow. Numbered connectors explain the handoffs; the last connects to recorded evidence and uncertainty. The rest state shows the whole sequence, while selecting a handoff exposes the relevant boundary. Best at teaching that review does not execute. More complex at narrow widths and could imply an implemented identity topology: role labels must remain conceptual, “where supported” stays visible, and the mobile ordered list must preserve the same meaning. No simulated signatures or approval stamps.

3. **Outcome lens.** A compact ordered five-stage spine occupies one side; the other keeps three equal-weight outcome explanations visible. Selecting a stage highlights which record helps answer the outcome question, without hiding uncertainty. Best for explaining why a request can stop and why unknown needs inspection. Weakest at initially explaining the main workflow, and easily mistaken for a status dashboard; avoid counts, badges, timestamps, progress meters or a selected “success” result. More suitable if evaluator feedback shows recovery is the main comprehension gap.

All are original layout concepts, not Hairline sample adaptations. Only the brief → alternatives → explicit selection → implementation → honest browser verification process is borrowed. No engine, figure, source SVG, package, asset or ten-rule implementation is selected. **A person must select one concept before implementation; no unattended fallback.** This document recommends no winning concept and asks for no immediate approval popup.

## Acceptance for a selected future pilot

- Static first: complete meaningful sequence and uncertainty explanation without animation, hover, JavaScript timing or a pointer. Essential authority/availability qualifications stay visible. No autoplay or required tour; motion is optional explanatory emphasis, never evidence of execution.
- Keyboard and touch: ordinary labelled stage buttons if selection is retained, Enter/Space activation and visible focus; preserve the current optional arrow/Home/End behavior and per-instance accessible relationships. No dragging, precise pointer gesture, hover-only content or touch-inaccessible readout. Selection only changes explanatory content and cannot call accounts/providers or persist approval/state.
- Screen readers: semantic ordered sequence, descriptive heading and associated explanation, meaningful outcome text. Decorative connectors are hidden from accessibility APIs. Selection must not move focus unexpectedly; no repeated decorative announcements. Essential content is available without interpreting geometry or color.
- Reduced motion: a complete static presentation with no pointer-follow response, parallax or automatic travel when reduced motion is requested. Normal-motion behavior, if later justified, must have a clear non-motion equivalent and stop/cleanup correctly. No animation library is presumed necessary.
- Responsive and readable: follow the existing IA checks at320/375/768/1024/1440px, 200% zoom and400% reflow; no clipped record labels or lost warnings. Text/controls and non-text states need measured applicable WCAG2.2AA contrast; use labels/shapes as well as color. Target at least24px controls with suitable spacing, prefer44px primary targets.
- Honest evidence: source/DOM tests can cover selection, focus, unique IDs and no operational calls, but do not establish browser acceptance. The selected milestone needs an actual supported-browser static/keyboard/touch/reduced-motion/responsive/screen-reader review and measured performance/cleanup evidence. Preserve existing drafts/sessions and use inert labelled data. Source/version and simulated boundaries accompany any capture; no synthetic UI passed off as the product.
